> **In short:**
> 1. Three formats need no measurement: a necklace with an extender chain, earrings and an adjustable ring.
> 2. Budget reads through the material: steel or gold coloured under 30 euros, sterling silver and gold plating between 30 and 80 euros, vermeil above 100 euros.
> 3. The best style indicator is what is already worn, the dominant metal above all.
> 4. The two riskiest formats are the fixed ring and the rigid bangle, where size cannot be corrected.

## Formats with no sizing risk

### The necklace

It is the safest gift. An **extender chain** of 3 to 5 cm moves the drop from one position to another, which absorbs most neck circumference differences. Lengths of 42 to 45 cm, falling just below the collarbone, suit the widest range of people.

### Earrings

They have no size. The only points of attention are the material for reactive skin, and weight if drop designs are not usual.

### The adjustable ring

Open at the back, it absorbs one to two sizes. It is the only ring format to give when the size is unknown.

## What each budget actually buys

| Budget | Accessible materials | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Under 30 euros | Steel, gold coloured metal | Limited lifespan in daily wear |
| 30 to 80 euros | Sterling silver, gold plated | Best durability to price ratio |
| Over 100 euros | Vermeil, set synthetic stones | Ages noticeably better |

Useful reminder: in France the term **gold plated** implies a gold layer of at least 3 microns, while gilded or flash plated guarantee no thickness.

## Reading the style already worn

Three clues are enough, and they can be observed without asking:

1. **The dominant metal.** Silver or gold coloured. Giving the metal the person never wears is the most common mistake.
2. **The scale of the pieces.** Discreet jewellery or bolder designs.
3. **The presence of stones.** Some people never wear any, and that is not an accident.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **The fixed ring with no measurement.** It fits or it does not, and the exchange ruins the effect.
- **The rigid bangle.** Same problem, plus the constraint of getting the hand through.
- **Engraving too early.** An engraving makes an exchange impossible.
- **A metal never worn.** A gold coloured necklace given to someone who only wears silver ends up in a box.

## Frequently asked questions

### What jewellery can you give for Mother's Day without knowing the size?

A necklace with an extender chain, earrings or an adjustable ring.

### What budget should you plan for a Mother's Day piece?

Between 30 and 80 euros to reach sterling silver and gold plating, the best durability to price ratio.

### How do you know which style to give?

By observing the dominant metal already worn, the scale of the pieces and whether stones are worn at all.
