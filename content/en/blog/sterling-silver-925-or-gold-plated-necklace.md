> **In short:**
> 1. In France, gold plated implies a gold layer of at least 3 microns, while gilded or flash plated guarantee no thickness.
> 2. Vermeil is gold plating over a sterling silver base, which makes wear far less of a problem than the same plating on base metal.
> 3. Sterling silver tarnishes reversibly, whereas gold plating wears away irreversibly.
> 4. A necklace wears more slowly than a ring or a bracelet, which are far more exposed to friction.

## What each term guarantees

| Term | Base | Gold layer | What happens as it wears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sterling silver 925 | 92.5% silver alloy | None | Tarnishes, cleans up |
| Vermeil | Sterling silver | At least 3 microns | Reveals silver |
| Gold plated | Base metal | At least 3 microns | Reveals the support metal |
| Gilded, flash plated | Base metal | Not guaranteed, very thin | Discolours quickly |

The decisive difference is not the brand-new look, identical to the eye in all four cases, but **what shows up once the layer wears**.

## Sterling silver: reversible ageing

Sterling silver 925 contains 92.5% pure silver, the remainder being an alloy meant to harden the metal, most often copper.

Its known flaw is **tarnishing**: in contact with sulphur compounds in the air, perspiration and cosmetics, a grey layer forms on the surface. It is neither wear nor a sign of fake silver, and a polishing cloth removes it.

In other words, a sterling silver necklace does not wear out, it dulls. That is maintenance, not end of life.

## Gold plating: irreversible ageing

The gold layer is a surface deposit. It thins with friction, and once gone it does not come back without a professional replating.

What accelerates wear:

- friction against skin, clothing and other jewellery,
- perspiration, slightly acidic,
- perfumes, hairspray and creams sprayed onto the piece,
- household products.

A **necklace** is less exposed than a ring or a bracelet, constantly in contact with whatever is being handled. It is the piece where gold plating lasts longest.

## Vermeil, the compromise

Vermeil applies the same minimum 3 micron plating, but over a **sterling silver** base instead of a base metal. Two consequences:

- as the gold layer wears, silver shows through, not an unknown alloy,
- skin tolerance stays that of silver, which matters for reactive skin.

For sensitive skin, the European REACH regulation also caps nickel release from jewellery in prolonged skin contact.

## How to choose

- **Daily wear, contained budget, maintenance accepted**: sterling silver.
- **Gold look and sensitive skin**: vermeil.
- **Occasional gold look, tight budget**: gold plated, accepting the wear.
- **Best avoided for daily wear**: anything described as gilded or flash plated, with no guaranteed thickness.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between gold plated and gilded?

Gold plated is a regulated term implying at least 3 microns of gold. Gilded or flash plated guarantee no thickness.

### What is vermeil?

Gold plating of at least 3 microns over a sterling silver base.

### Does gold plating wear off?

Yes, the gold layer is a surface deposit that thins irreversibly with friction.
