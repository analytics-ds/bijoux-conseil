> **In short:**
> 1. The ear splits into two zones that behave in opposite ways: the lobe, soft and well supplied with blood, and cartilage, rigid and slow to heal.
> 2. The commonly cited range is six to eight weeks for a lobe, against six to twelve months for cartilage.
> 3. Initial placement uses implant-grade material: ASTM F136 titanium, niobium or solid gold of at least 14 carats.
> 4. Not every placement works on every ear: the daith, the rook and the snug depend directly on anatomy.

## Placements, zone by zone

### On the lobe

The **lobe** is the fleshy lower part of the ear. With no cartilage and a good blood supply, it is the most forgiving zone. Second and third holes sit above the first, spaced far enough apart that the jewellery does not touch.

### On the cartilage

- **Helix**: along the upper outer rim, the most common cartilage placement.
- **Flat**: on the flat surface between the helix and the inner ear.
- **Conch**: in the central hollow, named after its shell shape.
- **Tragus**: on the small cartilage flap that shields the ear canal.
- **Anti-tragus**: on the ridge just above the lobe, facing the tragus.
- **Daith**: in the inner cartilage fold, above the canal.
- **Rook**: on the upper fold of the anti-helix.
- **Snug**: on the inner ridge, one of the most demanding placements.

## Healing time: ranges, not rules

| Placement | Tissue | Commonly cited range |
|---|---|---|
| Lobe | Flesh | 6 to 8 weeks |
| Helix, flat | Thin cartilage | 6 to 12 months |
| Conch, tragus | Cartilage | 6 to 12 months |
| Daith, rook, snug | Thick cartilage | 9 to 12 months and beyond |

These figures vary with the person, hygiene, placement quality and the jewellery used. Increasing pain, spreading redness or thick discharge call for medical advice, not an article.

## The jewellery matters as much as the placement

For an initial placement, professional piercer associations recommend implant-grade materials: titanium meeting **ASTM F136** or **ASTM F1295**, niobium, or solid gold of at least 14 carats. These materials limit nickel release, whose thresholds are set by the European REACH regulation, with the strictest limit for items inserted into a piercing.

Post length matters too: too short and it compresses the swelling of the first weeks, too long and it catches.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the different ear piercings?

The lobe takes the standard piercing and further holes. The cartilage takes the helix, flat, conch, tragus, anti-tragus, daith, rook and snug.

### Which ear piercing heals fastest?

The lobe, in a range of six to eight weeks, against six to twelve months for cartilage.

### What material should you choose for a fresh piercing?

ASTM F136 or F1295 titanium, niobium, or solid gold of at least 14 carats.
